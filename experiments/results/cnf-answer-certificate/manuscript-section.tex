\subsection{Current-answer projection with complete knowledge retained}
\label{sec:answer-certificate}

A learned archive serves two different future uses: checking the current answer
and supplying consequences for later edited inputs. We retain the entire archive
and construct a separate certificate for the first use. The original checker
validates every archive record before projection, including unused records.
For SAT, the projected certificate contains only the same model. For UNSAT, it
retains exactly the derived ancestors of the selected empty-clause conclusion,
in their original order, with remapped derived references. Original indices
still refer to the complete canonical source. Unknown produces an unknown
marker, with no answer or retained-knowledge authority. The result is then
checked against the original CNF by the unchanged checker.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Current-answer projection]
Given a valid resolution archive for a CNF $F$, this projection preserves its
checked answer and SAT witness, does not mutate the archive, and requires only
polynomial work in the explicit sizes of $F$ and the archive. It need not retain
a minimum refutation or establish a polynomial bound in $|F|$ alone.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
For an UNSAT result, the retained set is closed under every derived premise of
its selected conclusion. Original premises keep their original indices.
Process retained records in increasing original index order. By induction, all
premises have their former clauses, so each unchanged resolution pivot sequence
produces its former resolvent after index remapping. In particular, the empty
conclusion remains empty. A source empty clause is the zero-derivation case.
For SAT the identical witness is checked against $F$; for unknown no decision is
asserted. All retained lists and witness data are copied, so projection leaves
the archive intact. Checking the explicit records, traversing their dependency
graph, sorting retained indices, copying and checking the result have polynomial
cost in the explicit input and proof sizes. Those proof sizes are not bounded
here by a polynomial in the formula size.
\end{proof}

The experiment fixes the preceding sixteen edited targets and both stored search
traces for each, without rerunning discovery or changing admission. Its primary
comparison is the reused arm's five SAT and eleven UNSAT results; the fresh arm
has three SAT, six UNSAT and seven unknowns. Both full and projected controls
use the same compact JSON encoder and per-case gzip. This differs from the prior
indented JSON representation; only matched columns are comparable. Two reversed
pairs of fresh processes read, hash, decompress and check one arm with search,
transport and projection disabled. OS caches are not flushed. Construction and
its full-archive checks, compression, writes and memory are charged separately;
old discovery, parent acquisition and retained storage remain additional.

On the reused stratum, derived records fall from 87,054 to 46,960,
compact JSON bytes from 26,364,575 to 14,107,112, and per-case
gzip bytes from 4,964,077 to 2,740,517. All sixteen answers remain.
Mean read/hash/decompress/check time falls from 4.77053 to
2.59003 seconds (45.71\%). SAT replay changes from
1.30999 to 0.00816 seconds and UNSAT replay from
3.46054 to 2.58187 seconds. Sidecar preparation costs
9.79502 seconds, including both archive and output validation;
the measured per-case saving repays it after about five repeated replays.
Shared setup and process startup are additional to that estimate. The old
132,091,258-byte indented archives remain, and gzip answer sidecars add
2,740,517 bytes. This is not an end-to-end solver speedup or a reduction
in total retained storage. The fresh stratum retains three SAT, six UNSAT and
seven unknowns, whose marker-only savings are reported separately.

Across all 32 traces, mean fresh-process wall time is
7.24189 versus 3.24801 seconds, with peak RSS
96,550,912 versus 87,851,008 bytes. Single-run preparation of
both controls takes 17.93170 seconds and peaks at
207,929,344 bytes. Four fresh workers reproduce answers and
checker counters. All 2,708 full and eleven focused tests pass; validation
preserves 255 manifests, 85,130 prior artifact entries, 1,384 scientific sources,
all 92 earlier proof blocks and three published snapshots. Four scientific
files and the projection proof above are added.

The projection is an answer artifact, not a replacement knowledge archive.
Its unknown marker cannot be counted as a solved query. The closure is relative
to a recorded proof, not a minimum support or semantic characterization of all
necessary clauses. The independent finite audit checks 7,760 projections on
3,880 formulas using exhaustive model enumeration and a reverse dependency scan.
It covers every two-variable clause set, three-variable sets with at most three
clauses, sparse signed mixtures, complete and capped traces, and deliberately
appended valid unused records. Corrupt unused records are rejected before
projection. Broader distribution coverage, smaller retained archives, alternative
supports, preprocessing and efficient unrestricted expansion remain open.
