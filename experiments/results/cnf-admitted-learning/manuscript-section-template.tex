\subsection{Bounding active knowledge without discarding proof evidence}
\label{sec:admitted-learning}

The selective transport preserves valid knowledge after edits, but activating
all surviving clauses made the measured expansion substantially more expensive.
The next experiment separates logical availability from active search input.
Every surviving derivation is still checked, copied and retained.  Only a bounded
subset of its claimed clauses is added to the target's propagation input.
No production default or proof format changes.

The frozen policy admits at most 128 distinct learned clauses not already
present as target clauses.  Smaller literal width ranks first; more recent
retained derivations break ties.  Duplicate learned clauses use their most
recent record.  Ranking uses proof syntax, not target truth.  It is a heuristic
for economical admission, not a claim that short clauses always give better
search.  The initial complete proposal still comes from the target alone,
and both policies use the unchanged learner with 2,000 entered contexts.
The cap applies only to imported active knowledge, not clauses subsequently
learned during expansion.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded active knowledge with complete proof retention]
Let $F$ be a target CNF and let $K$ be any collection of clauses with checked
resolution derivations from $F$.  For any selected subset $A\subseteq K$,
including the empty subset, $F$ and $F\land\bigwedge A$ have exactly the same
models.  Inactive clauses in $K\setminus A$ and their derivations may remain in
the retained certificate and serve as ancestors of active-clause proofs without
being inserted into the search input.  Exact substitution of those proofs
converts a refutation of the augmented formula into a refutation of $F$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every clause in $K$ is implied by $F$, so every model of $F$ satisfies every
member of $A$.  The augmented formula also contains $F$, which gives the reverse
model inclusion.  For proof substitution, place all retained derivations before
the new derivations and map each augmented premise to a target clause or its
checked earlier derivation.  The topological resolution order remains valid.
An ancestor's presence in this certificate does not require its insertion into
the search clause set.  The unchanged checker reconstructs every claimed clause
and any final empty clause from $F$.  A SAT answer still requires a complete
target model; an incomplete search remains unknown.
\end{proof}

This result justifies any admission subset, not the particular ranking's
performance.  Old proofs, selective transport, ranking and full retained evidence
are all charged.  Bounding the active import does not bound the size of its
proof support, newly acquired clauses or unrestricted search.  Proof pruning,
watched-literal propagation and alternative-support discovery remain separate
work.  Inactive evidence is preserved, including for capped outcomes.

ADMISSION_MEASUREMENTS

A post-hoc trace of the earlier refutations counts distinct retained clauses
used directly by reachable new derivations.  Lost cases \texttt{s021} and
\texttt{s031} use 630 and 292 such clauses; the candidate admits only 122 and
95, omitting 508 and 197.  These are properties of the recorded proof routes,
not clauses necessary in all possible refutations.  Indeed, \texttt{s023}
still succeeds despite admitting only 112 of the 578 clauses on its baseline
route.  Different search can obtain a different proof.  This motivates testing
use-informed admission or cheaper propagation with broader clause access; it
does not establish a causal diagnosis of each capped outcome.

The reproduced small-case audit checks 13,624 edited targets from all 512
two-variable clause sets, 3,304 three-variable clause sets of at most three
clauses, and 128 fresh sparse signed mixtures.  Admission budgets 0, 1 and 128
receive independent ranking checks and complete model-set comparisons, giving
40,872 admission checks, model-set equalities and composed-answer checks:
32,853 SAT and 8,019 UNSAT.  Every output preserves the complete retained
proof prefix.  At zero expansion budget, 11,285 cases remain unknown and the
other 2,339 retain checked refutations.  Repeated budgets and related targets
are not independent problem draws.

All 2,659 full and 19 focused tests pass.  Tests cover duplicate claims, target
clauses excluded from admission, newest-first ties, the 128-clause cap, corrupted
inactive evidence, zero budgets, exact substitution, target additions and
checking without search.  The initial test-fixture error log is preserved:
four tests supplied old clause indices directly to an edited target and were
correctly rejected.  Remapping those fixtures through checked transport fixes
the tests; a dedicated rejection test retains the counterexample.

Validation reruns both measured policies, reproduces their certificates and
counters, and checks all saved compression and replay data.  The full-admission
baseline reproduces its previous checkpoint exactly.  All 251 earlier manifests,
84,515 artifact entries, 1,367 scientific sources and 90 preceding proof blocks
remain unchanged; five scientific files and one proof are added.  Historical
coverage, unknowns, counterexamples and cumulative tables remain separately
preserved.  General preprocessing, efficient recognition and unrestricted SAT
expansion remain open; finite edited-input results do not establish a polynomial
SAT algorithm.
