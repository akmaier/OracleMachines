\subsection{Exact separator messages for cyclic signed choices}
\label{sec:choice-separator}

The preceding forest rule fails on five exposed demand graphs even after the best
two-vertex removals. We retain the same checked signed-demand and binary-exclusion
syntax, but now allow cycles inside elimination bags. The single-representative
normalization of Section~\ref{sec:choice-forest} still applies. Discovery proposes
a deterministic minimum-fill order: minimize missing pairs among current
neighbors, then degree, then demand index. Fill neighbors into a clique before
removing a vertex. This heuristic is not an optimal-width algorithm.

The checker accepts the order, not trusted bags. It rebuilds elimination bags
from every original demand-graph edge. At step $i$, the eliminated demand $v_i$
and its later filled neighbors form a bag; those neighbors are separator $S_i$.
A message is assigned to the earliest later vertex of its separator, or becomes
a root message when that separator is empty. This construction covers every
original edge and has running intersection. The checker also explicitly verifies
that each child-message scope lies inside its parent bag. Messages describe
complete feasible relations on their separators, including excluded tuples.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Exact elimination-message authority]
For a fully checked signed-choice source and elimination order, let $R_i$ contain
exactly the separator assignments that extend to a value of $v_i$ satisfying its
original exclusions to later demands and every incoming message. Any verified
empty $R_i$ proves UNSAT, even in an incomplete message prefix. Otherwise a
proper prefix is unknown; complete nonempty messages admit a satisfying source
model reconstructed in reverse order.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
Treat exclusions as binary factors on representative choices, with their domains
supplied by the demands. Assign an original exclusion to its earlier-eliminated
endpoint. At step $i$, all active factors containing $v_i$ are its still-relevant
original exclusions and the messages assigned to its bucket. Their scopes lie
in $\{v_i\}\cup S_i$ by the filled-graph construction. Replace their conjunction
by its existential projection over $v_i$. This is precisely $R_i$, since the
checker tests every separator tuple for an extending value and checks both
present and absent rows. All other active factors are independent of $v_i$.
Induction therefore preserves the conjunction's satisfiability under elimination.
An empty factor makes that conjunction false, regardless of unprocessed factors,
so an empty verified message already proves UNSAT. No such conclusion follows
from nonempty messages in a proper prefix.

After complete elimination every root message is on an empty separator and is
true exactly when its sole empty tuple is present. If all messages are nonempty,
choose an extending value at each step in reverse order. The checked existential
equations guarantee an extension compatible with the selected separator values
and child messages. This reconstructs compatible representatives for all demands.
The preceding normalization lifts them to a Boolean assignment; the implementation
also checks that model against every raw source clause and its full variable
support. This proves SAT without assuming that the order heuristic is complete
or that the bag width is small.
\end{proof}

Write $B_i=|D_{v_i}|\prod_{j\in S_i}|D_j|$. Enumerating all bag assignments gives
an upper bound proportional to
\[
 \sum_i B_i\left(1+|S_i|+\sum_{c\in\operatorname{children}(i)}|S_c|\right)
\]
for local compatibility and message lookup work, apart from input preparation,
graph construction, sorting, persistence and raw-model verification. Actual
search may stop at the first extending value for a tuple. Retained relations
can be smaller than the enumerated space, but verification also checks omitted
tuples. A short empty relation is therefore not a claim of polynomial-time
verification for unrestricted width. Bounded separator width gives the familiar
finite-domain dynamic-programming bound; unrestricted width can be exponential.
The contribution here is checked integration and a measured structural boundary,
not a new general tractability theorem or novelty of elimination.

The frozen experimental limits are 200,000 reserved bag assignments in total,
65,536 per bag, and 1,000,000 ordering pair tests. Each complete message reserves
its whole $B_i$ before construction; a stopped attempt retains only complete
messages. Checking and graph/source preparation have separate recorded costs.
These caps are not total lifetime or memory bounds. A graph-only pre-freeze pilot
on the five exposed cases found separator sizes 6--9, largest bags of
2,187--59,049 assignments, and full plans of 15,042--141,654 assignments.
The caps deliberately admit these exposed descriptions. Fresh controls are
therefore required before discussing transfer. Greedy demand extraction remains
incomplete; failure of a proposal is not exclusion of all possible descriptions.

The measurement retains all 78 previous cases and adds sixteen signed sparse
controls generated only after the source/protocol freeze: ladders, triangle
strips, mixed-domain ladders and ternary edits at 8, 16, 24 and 32 demands.
Inputs are unlabelled to both algorithms, and no outcomes are omitted. The old
external panel contains 25 originals and twelve derived controls. The five
nonclique cases are four original flat30 formulas and one signed permutation;
they are exposed, not five independent fresh external observations.

The new route checks all five retained nonclique sources. Their maximum separator
sizes are 8, 7, 9, 6 and 8; full reserved assignment counts are 70,140,
38,640, 141,654, 15,042 and 85,206. Each completes thirty messages, with
5,794, 3,211, 10,255, 2,704 and 5,845 retained relation rows, respectively.
Their previous SAT witnesses remain valid: this gives an exact structural
explanation, not new historical portfolio answers. On the exposed controls,
the additional four results are $K_5$ refutations, yielding nineteen SAT and
seventeen UNSAT results. The fresh controls yield twelve SAT results, including
nine separator-only cases, three shared cases and four jointly unresolved cases.
Across the 94 inputs, there are 36 shared answers, eighteen separator-only answers
and forty jointly unresolved cases; none is two-cut-only. This is not a universal
dominance claim under all possible limits and source descriptions.

The unresolved cases separate into 26 external inputs with unclassified clauses,
six external inputs stopped by the per-bag limit, and eight unsupported ternary
controls (four exposed, four fresh). Source classification failure concerns the
proposed descriptions, not a proof excluding every alternative description.
Dense clique-capacity cases retain their earlier specialized proofs. Next we
will test source-bound separator tables beyond the signed-choice grammar, with
full Boolean semantics and resulting width made explicit. Higher-arity exclusions
preserve the representative normalization; arbitrary positive interactions need
a separate proof or full Boolean domains. Alternative orders, cross-edit message
reuse, preprocessing and efficient unrestricted SAT remain open.

Two construction orders and four constructor-disabled cold replays reproduce
all certificates, answers and checker counters. On the complete 94-input panel,
mean discovery is 0.12029 seconds for two-cut forests and 1.56959 for separators;
further checking is 0.01264 versus 0.76843 seconds. Encoding, gzip and writing
cost 0.00922 versus 0.07359 seconds; process wall time is 0.23418 versus
2.50316 seconds. Compact JSON occupies 45,520 versus 753,180 bytes and gzip
13,179 versus 111,578 bytes. Unknown markers and incomplete exact prefixes
are included in storage but not counted as answers. More checked results cost
more; these numbers are not a speedup comparison.

Cold worker replay costs 0.02011 versus 0.79480 seconds, with process wall
costs 0.09876 versus 0.87459 seconds. Construction peak RSS is 41,402,368
versus 46,612,480 bytes; replay peaks at 40,878,080 versus 44,859,392 bytes.
OS caches are not flushed. Prior acquisition, independent audits and retained
historical storage remain additional. Complete structural replay has a different
purpose and cost from checking an already available SAT witness alone.

All 2,739 full and sixteen focused tests pass. Independent audits reproduce
3,880 small CNFs, 24,576 separator-message projections and 32,768 prefix
verdicts across all 4,096 cross-conflict subsets of three binary demands, using
two reversed orders and alternating internal exclusions. Each message is checked
against brute-force existential projection of its raw-constraint subtree.
Validation preserves 257 earlier manifests, 85,720 artifact entries, 1,392
scientific sources, all 94 earlier proof blocks and three publication snapshots.
Four frozen scientific files and the proposition above extend this record.
